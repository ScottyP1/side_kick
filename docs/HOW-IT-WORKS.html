How Sidekick works

One app, a handful of cloud services, and Microsoft Teams. This page follows a new client from sign-up to their first meeting notes, then traces a question and a meeting through the system, and shows the walls that keep each client's data apart.

1 · Services map

One app in the middle, everything else is a service it calls

Sidekick is a single Next.js application. The same container is the public website, the customer portal, your Owner Console, the API and the Teams bot. There's no separate "backend" to keep in sync.

2 · New client setup

From sign-up to the first notes in Teams

Three kinds of people do the setup, in this order. Most steps happen once per client company. The made-up client here is Northwind Consulting, whose engagement team works for its client Contoso.

  1. Sign up and pick a plan Org Owner

    The company signs up at /signup (name, owner, plan, free trial, then checkout) and the setup wizard follows. Self-service sign-up is off for now, so today the Sidekick team creates each client; flipping it on is one switch in the Owner Console. Plans are priced per group: Starter $199 (1 group), Team $699 (5), Business $1,999 (20), Enterprise custom.

  2. Connect Microsoft sign-in Org Owner

    Settings → Microsoft sign-in → Connect with Microsoft. This ties the Sidekick company to the client's Microsoft 365 tenant. From then on people use "Sign in with Microsoft", and on Business and up the owner can require it (no Sidekick passwords left).

  3. The client's Microsoft admin approves Sidekick, once Microsoft admin

    An Org Owner or Admin starts it from Users & access → Approve Sidekick for your organization; a Global Administrator (or Cloud Application Administrator) approves on Microsoft's page. What they approve, in Sidekick's own words:

PermissionWhy Sidekick needs it
User.Read (delegated)Lets people sign in with their work account. Sidekick sees name, email and account id.
User.Read.AllSearch the directory when an admin imports people or someone types @Sidekick add. Names, work emails, job titles and ids only; never changes anything.

  1. Create groups and add people Org Owner / Admin

    One group per client team (e.g. "Contoso cutover", client Contoso, Team Lead Priya Raman). People are added by email or imported from Microsoft 365, each with a role: Org Owner, Org Admin, Team Lead, Member or Viewer. Team Leads can import into their own groups.

  2. One-time Teams admin settings Microsoft / Teams admin

    In the client's Teams admin center and Teams PowerShell (we send them RUNBOOKS → Automatic meeting notes):

    • An app permission policy that lets only Team Leads and admins add Sidekick (Sidekick enforces this itself too, this just avoids "joined and left" noise).
    • Resource-specific consent for chats on, so Sidekick can read the chats it's assigned to.
    • An application access policy for Sidekick, transcription on in the meeting policy, and Transcript API access on (off by default since 2026; without it Microsoft refuses every transcript).

  3. Add Sidekick to a Teams group chat Team Lead

    Only a Team Lead, Org Admin or Org Owner may add it. Sidekick checks the adder's Microsoft account (and looks them up in the directory by email if needed); anyone else gets a short card and Sidekick leaves. It posts "I'm not set up for this chat yet" and then reads, saves and answers nothing.

  4. Assign the chat to one group, in the portal Team Lead / Admin

    Group page → Teams chats → Assign a chat lists waiting chats with who added Sidekick, when, and who's in it. Tick "client guests" if the client can read the chat. Assigning happens only in the portal, never from Teams. Unassigned after 7 days, Sidekick leaves.

  5. Choose where meeting notes go Team Lead

    Group → Teams chats → "Meeting notes go to": one chat (usual), several, or "Don't post notes in Teams". Also from the chat: @Sidekick post meeting notes here. Until someone chooses, notes go to one chat (one named "notes", else the first without client guests).

  6. Turn on automatic notes Org Owner / Admin

    Meetings → Automatic notes after Teams meetings → Turn on. Sidekick checks the setup straight away; if something is missing (approval, access policy, Transcript API access) the panel names it in plain words.

  7. First notes arrive Sidekick

    After the group's next transcribed Teams meeting, usually within minutes (at most about 15), the notes card lands in the chosen chat. Section 4 shows how.

3 · A question's journey

"When is the wave 2 cutover?"

The same path runs whether the question comes from @Sidekick in a Teams chat or from Ask in the portal (ask() in lib/sidekick.ts). Every stop can end the trip early, and each early ending has fixed wording.

The walls come first

In the portal, requireWorkspace refuses any group the person isn't a member of (admins see every group in their own company). In Teams, the chat itself decides the group: an assigned chat belongs to exactly one group. Unassigned chats get no answers at all.

Guardrails on the way in

  • Quick local check: no AI, no cost. Slurs, strong swearing, known jailbreak phrases.
  • Claude check: a small call judges the question against the locked rules (no hate, harassment, sexual content, harm; can't be talked out of its rules; never crosses client walls; "I don't know" instead of guessing), the switches the Org Owner turned on (swearing, small talk, judging people, HR/legal/medical, contact details, competitors) and up to 20 custom rules ("Never quote day rates").
  • Switches and custom rules can only make Sidekick stricter. A group can add switches on top of the company's, never remove them.
  • Follow-ups ("and wave 3?") are rewritten into a full question first, and both versions are checked.

Search and ranking

Looks only in this group's documents, meetings, notes, chats, connected tools and the company's firm library (lib/search.ts). With client guests present, only Shareable sources. About 30 candidates are re-ranked (lib/rank.ts): fit to the whole question, freshness, outdated versions ×0.55, firm policy first for policy questions, meeting notes ×1.15. Duplicates are dropped, at most 3 passages per source, the best 10 go to Claude.

On the way out

  • Grounding: the answer must cite a source that was actually sent. If not, it becomes "I don't know. I couldn't back that up…" and the question is logged as a knowledge gap.
  • Swearing masked, a slur replaces the whole answer, emails and phone numbers hidden when that switch is on.
  • If sources disagree, Claude says so ("the sources disagree").
4 · A meeting's journey

From "End meeting" to notes in the chat

No bot joins the call. Sidekick reads the transcript Teams already makes, through Microsoft Graph, after the meeting ends (lib/meetings-graph.ts). This covers scheduled Teams meetings today; "Meet now" calls started from a group chat are being added.

Which group gets the meeting

  • If Sidekick is in the meeting's own chat and that chat is assigned, that group.
  • Otherwise the group with the most of its people among organizer and invitees, at least two.
  • A real tie waits under Pick a group for an admin or one of the Team Leads.
  • Not any group's meeting: skipped, and not even the title is kept.

Client guests

If anyone from another Microsoft organization attended, the meeting is marked Client guests present and its notes count as shareable. Notes from internal meetings are never posted to a chat with client guests, whatever is ticked.

Save to knowledge

Decisions, requirements, risks and facts on the card can be saved to the group's knowledge with one click; answers then find them first, long after the meeting.

5 · The walls

What keeps one client's data away from another

There are two walls: between companies (organizations), and between groups inside a company. Every query in the code carries both the company and the group; an automated test tries to cross each wall on every new feature.

How the walls are built

  • Every API route that touches group data calls requireWorkspace first. Not a member? "Not found", so it doesn't even confirm the group exists.
  • Search filters every query to one company and one group (plus that company's firm library), in Postgres and in Azure AI Search alike.
  • A Teams chat is assigned to exactly one group, only in the portal, only by a Team Lead or admin.
  • Claude only ever sees the handful of passages for one question, and is told to treat them as data, never instructions.

Guests and Teams

  • Someone in an assigned chat who isn't a Sidekick person acts as a Member of that one group, shown as "Name (Teams)".
  • Chats marked "client guests" get answers from Shareable sources only, and only notes from meetings the client attended.
  • Uploads are checked for another client's name before they're searchable; a likely mistake is held until someone decides.
  • When an engagement ends, a group can roll off: read-only, then deleted with a deletion certificate.
6 · Questions you might get asked

Short answers

Where does our data live?
In Sidekick's Postgres database in Microsoft Azure (Central US), with 35 days of backups. A searchable copy of passages sits in Azure AI Search. Uploaded files are kept as text; original files aren't stored. Claude only receives the few passages needed for one answer or one transcript, stores nothing between calls, and Anthropic doesn't train on API data by default.
Does a bot join our calls?
No. Sidekick reads the transcript Teams already makes, after the meeting ends. Nothing joins, listens or records. (A live in-meeting Sidekick is a planned Enterprise feature.)
What Microsoft permissions does it need?
Sign-in, read the directory (names, emails, titles), remove itself from chats, and read meeting details and transcripts once automatic notes are on. All read-only towards your data; it never changes anything in Microsoft 365. Chat messages are granted per chat and read only once a chat is assigned to a group. Full list in section 2.
Who can add Sidekick to a chat?
Only Team Leads, Org Admins and the Org Owner. If anyone else adds it, Sidekick says so and leaves. A chat nobody assigns within 7 days is left automatically.
Could another client see our information?
No. Every query is walled to one company and one group, Teams chats belong to exactly one group, and an automated test tries to break those walls on every change. See section 5.
What if Claude is wrong?
Every answer cites its sources, so people can check. If no real source backs the answer, Sidekick says "I don't know" instead of guessing and logs the question as a knowledge gap. If sources disagree, it says so. People can mark answers 👍/👎, and admins see the counts.
What does it cost to run?
About $45 a month of Azure today (app, database, registry, logs). Before the first real customer, search moves to the Basic tier (about $75 a month). At around 100 companies the Azure plan is roughly $500–600 a month. On top of that: Claude, billed by Anthropic per use and the biggest variable cost, and Microsoft's transcript fee of about $0.0022 per meeting minute (about $6.60 per 100 half-hour meetings), paid by Sidekick.
What happens if something breaks?
Search falls back to its built-in index; Claude being busy shows a plain "try again in a minute"; missed meetings are caught by the 15-minute poll; background jobs retry and land in a dead-letter list you can see in the Owner Console. A bad deploy rolls itself back.
Can we control what it will answer?
Yes. Locked rules always apply; the Org Owner adds switches (no HR/legal/medical advice, no competitor talk, hide contact details…) and up to 20 custom rules. A group can be made stricter than the rest. Rules can only tighten, never loosen.
What happens when an engagement ends?
The group is set to end: it becomes read-only, can be exported as a zip, and is deleted after the notice period with a deletion certificate. Canceling the whole account does the same for the company.
